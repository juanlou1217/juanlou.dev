# Reflection writing reference

Use this reference only for life, work, or growth reflections, especially drafts assembled from spoken notes.

## Preserve the live voice

Find the emotional trigger, main self-recognition, outside idea or framework, and real next action before restructuring. Preserve concrete scenes, reactions, hesitation, excitement, embarrassment, self-correction, and surprising turns that reveal the author's actual state.

Clean obvious speech artifacts and repetition, but do not translate vivid language into cold abstractions. A reflection may be clear without sounding like a formal essay or retrospective report.

For an existing piece, begin with sentence-level repairs and bridge transitions. Replace whole sections only when the user asked for a rewrite or the original structure prevents the central idea from landing.

## Shape the material

Choose one visible route, such as:

- feeling → recognition → action
- self → environment → breakthrough
- event → interpretation → next step
- conversation → personal understanding → experiment

Use two to four `##` sections for that main movement. Use `###` only for supporting ideas. Do not make every heading sound like a final lesson, and do not give equal space to every minor thought.

Keep representative original-feeling sentences in each major section. If the revision becomes too smooth or generic, restore a real scene, name, reaction, or unfinished thought from the source notes rather than inventing new experience.

## Final check

- The central emotional movement is visible.
- The strongest original observations remain.
- External frameworks support the story instead of taking it over.
- The ending contains an honest implication or action, not a manufactured inspirational slogan.
- The piece still sounds like the author when read aloud.
